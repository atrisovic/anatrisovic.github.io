This page collects my October 2026 conference papers and the research question that connects them.

Research question

How does science actually use AI foundation models, and what do the scale, funding, and lifespan of the models scientists adopt mean for the independence and durability of research?

The papers below each answer part of this question. The Shrinking Lifespan of LLMs in Science follows adopted models over time and shows that each successive release year compresses how long a model matters. Open Science, Closed Models links funding acknowledgments to whether papers extend, use, or merely reference models, and finds that the gap across research systems is one of control rather than adoption. The Rapid Growth of AI Foundation Model Usage in Science measures use directly, separating papers that discuss a model from those that run or adapt one, and shows adoption rising near-exponentially while the models scientists use lag far behind the AI frontier.

Papers

The Shrinking Lifespan of LLMs in Science

Ana Trišović.
Conference on Language Modeling (COLM), 2026.

Key finding: Each successive release year is associated with a 27% shorter time-to-peak and a 23% shorter lifespan (p < 0.001), robust to minimum-age thresholds and controls for model size.

Open Science, Closed Models: How Funding Shapes AI in Science

Ana Trišović, Janakan Sivaloganathan.
AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society (AIES), 2026.

Key finding: Across 104,226 papers (2018–2025), the gap across research systems is not one of AI adoption but of control: off-the-shelf use is rising across fields and country groups, and the least publicly funded group, the Global South, now has the highest share of API-only engagement (0.4% to 10.0% of engaged papers).

The Rapid Growth of AI Foundation Model Usage in Science

Ana Trišović†, Alex Fogelson†, Janakan Sivaloganathan, Neil Thompson. (†These authors share first authorship.)
arXiv preprint, 2025.

Key finding: Citing a foundation model proves nearly three times as common as using one; adoption has nonetheless grown near-exponentially, reaching well over one percent of all publications by 2024, yet the science built on these models runs far behind the AI frontier.
